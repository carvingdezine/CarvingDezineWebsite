Foundations

Brand Architecture

A parent brand with a distinct project identity. Vraj Shyam Group leads; Vraj Imperia carries forward — each with its own mark, both drawn from the same palette and typefaces, so they read as one family.

Parent Brand

Vraj Shyam Group

Real Estate Developer · Established 1984

The endorsing brand. Appears on letterhead, company profile, site boards, and as the trust signature on every project's collateral. Its geometric crystal mark — a faceted form encoding four decades of architectural precision — is its primary device. A forty-year mark drawn from the same earth it builds on.

Current Project

Vraj Imperia

Mixed Use · Retail + Office · Waghodia Road

A 4-floor commercial development, 42 units, launching April 2026. Its wordmark is paired with an architectural icon abstracted from the building's own facade — fins, brick, vertical rhythm. On all Imperia collateral the VSG mark is always present as endorsement, below or alongside the project mark, never above it.

Every project brand carries the endorsement "by Vraj Shyam Group" on all sales material — connecting every new project to 40 years of delivered trust. This line is mandatory. It is never hidden, never below minimum size, never omitted.

Charcoal is not black
Both logo marks use Charcoal #2E2E2E — a deep warm brown — never pure black #000000. In print, build Charcoal as CMYK 0/0/0/82 (Pantone Black 6 C). Never use a 4-colour rich black build on fine text; single K only. Pure black kills the warmth of the system and makes Brick Red read too saturated by contrast.
Brick is dusty, not bright
Brick Red #B85C38 is muted and matte — taken from kiln-fired brick under an Indian sky. In print: CMYK 18/75/85/7 · Pantone 7586 C. Always request a wet proof before press approval. If the swatch returns glossy, orange-shifted, or over-saturated, reject and reprint. The ink should look dry and earthen, not vivid.
Stone — warm grey, not cool
Stone #8C7B6E is a warm brownish grey — CMYK 0/12/21/45 · Pantone Warm Gray 8 C. Never substitute a neutral or cool grey. The yellow and magenta ink components are intentional; they give the grey its warmth and connect it visually to the Brick Red.
Off-White — visible warmth on paper
Off-White #F0EBE3 is the paper stock colour as much as an ink colour — CMYK 0/2/5/6 · Pantone 9180 C. When specifying paper for print, match the stock swatch to Pantone 9180 C at proof. A stock that reads bright white in hand means the paper is wrong; request a warmer uncoated sheet.

Visual Direction

Design Language

Two visual signatures separate this brand from generic real estate work: a diagonal cut that organises every layout, and a downward-pointing triangle that holds every photograph. Together they echo the geometry already inside the crystal mark.

Content Zone
Cols 1–6
Off-White
Visual Zone · Cols 7–12
Diagonal col 7 → col 10 · 25% shift · ≈ 30°
The Diagonal

Angled cuts, not rectangles

One structural diagonal runs through every layout — a single grid-anchored boundary that starts at the top of column 7 (50% of layout width) and ends at column 10 at the bottom (75%). This divides the Content Zone (cols 1–6, Off-White) from the Visual Zone (cols 7–12, Charcoal Dark) in every brand asset. The 25% shift creates a ≈ 30° angle. This is not a decorative element — it is the structural grid.

The Triangle

Photographs cropped to a downward V

Every photograph in the system — in the profile, brochure, on hoardings — is masked into a downward-pointing triangle with sharp die-cut edges. The off-white paper remains visible in the corners outside the triangle. This is intentional negative space; it must never be filled in or softened to a rectangle. The triangle echoes the geometry of the logo mark.

Hard rule — no exceptions
No rounded corners — anywhere. Cards, documents, photo crops, panels, and buttons all use sharp 90° corners. Roundness reads as soft, generic, and web-app default. This brand is architectural, and architecture has edges.
06
Visual Direction

Photography

Bright, airy, warm — premium earthy editorial. Reference: Aesop campaign photography, Good Earth catalogue, Indian heritage architecture at golden hour. Never moody, never dramatic, never over-styled.

A
Light
A single warm natural source from upper left — late afternoon window light quality. Soft diffused shadows. Never a softbox grid, never harsh strobe, never fluorescent. The image should feel like 4:30pm on a clear day.
B
Lens & Depth
50mm equivalent at f/2.2. Shallow depth of field with a calm subject in focus and the surrounding surface dropping gently out. No fisheye, no extreme tele, no in-camera filters.
C
Surfaces
Stationery shots: dark honed black granite beneath the printed piece. Outdoor and site assets: real Indian environments — paving, scaffolding, evening street. Marble with veining, white linen, polished glass, and polished wood are out of system.
D
Mood
Calm, grounded, refined. The image should feel earned — like a developer with forty years to point to, not a startup launching its first asset. No motion, no people gesturing at camera, no lifestyle stock.

Visual Direction

Print Standards

Every printed piece follows the same three rules: matte finish, warm paper, ink-flat colour. Reject any proof that returns outside these parameters.

01
Finish
Flat matte only
No spot UV, no foil, no metallic ink, no embossing, no varnish. The whole system prints dry. Texture comes from the paper, not the finish.
02
Paper
Warm uncoated stock
300 GSM uncoated cotton-blend for cards. 120 GSM matte uncoated for letterhead. The stock should be visibly off-white in hand — match swatch #F0EBE3 at proof.
03
Ink
Flat dusty brick
Brick Red as flat, slightly dusty matte ink. Pantone 7586 C is the reference. CMYK build: 18 / 75 / 85 / 7. If the ink returns bright, glossy, or orange-shifted — reprint. See Colour Palette section for all five colours' full CMYK and Pantone references.

Web System

Website Design System

Every rule from the print system carries directly to the web — Cinzel for all headings, DM Sans for all body copy, the five-colour palette, sharp 90° corners on every component, and the diagonal used as a structural device wherever space allows. The web introduces one addition: interactive states. Everything else is already decided.

Core principle
The website is the print system in motion. Cinzel headings, DM Sans body, the five brand colours, and sharp 90° corners apply to every element without exception. No rounded corners, no pure black, no web-default blues for links. Browser default styles must be completely overridden.

Card hover rule
Card hover adds box-shadow only — never translate/lift the card, never change the background. Shadow: 0 16px 48px -12px rgba(46,46,46,.18). The brand is grounded; cards do not float.

Section Patterns

The diagonal from the print system translates directly to web CTAs and hero sections — using the same 12-column clip-path geometry. The triangle appears as a photo crop and as a geometric device inside dark sections.

Section usage rules
Section BGs
Alternate: Off-White → Off-White Soft → Charcoal Dark → Off-White. Never two Charcoal Dark sections back-to-back without an Off-White break.
Diagonal in web
Applies to hero sections and CTA banners using the same clip-path: polygon(0% 0%, 100% 0%, 100% 100%, 25% 100%). Only used when a full-bleed visual panel is present — not on text-only sections.
Triangle device
Used inside dark sections as a geometric accent, and as a photo crop mask on all project images. clip-path: polygon(50% 100%, 0% 0%, 100% 0%)
Section heading
Always: eyebrow (DM Sans 600 caps, Brick) → H2 (Cinzel 500) → lede (DM Sans 300, max-width 640px). Never skip the eyebrow on a hero or landing section.
Max width
Section content max-width: 1280px · margin: 0 auto · padding: 0 80px desktop, 0 24px mobile.

Implementation notes
