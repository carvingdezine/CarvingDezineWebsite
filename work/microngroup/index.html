Step 01 · Engineering
🔍
Technical Evaluation

No commitment before feasibility is confirmed. Every inquiry starts with a formal review of drawings, tolerances, material specifications, and production volumes.

Drawing and specification review against process capabilities
Material compatibility and grade validation for the application
Process fit assessment — moulding, machining, or hybrid route
Initial risk identification and volume feasibility check
Step 02 · Engineering
📐
DFM & Feasibility

Design for Manufacturability analysis, Moldflow simulation, and material validation — completed in-house and shared with the client before tooling is committed.

Moldflow simulation — warp, fill, sink, and gate location analysis
DFM report flagging draft angles, wall thickness, and parting concerns
Material substitution recommendations where applicable
Costing baseline shared — tooling, piece part, and amortization
Step 03 · Engineering
⚙️
Tooling Development

In-house CNC machining and Wire EDM. Tool design executed by Micron engineers — not outsourced. Milestone-tracked with client updates at each gate.

Tool design on UG/CATIA with cavity, core, and cooling layout
CNC roughing and finishing — on Micron's in-house machines
Wire EDM for complex inserts, lifters, and fine detail features
Client gate sign-off at tool design stage before steel is cut
Step 04 · Engineering
🧪
Trial & Validation

T0, T1, T2 moulding trials with dimensional check via CMM after each stage. Process parameters locked only after client sign-off on approved samples.

T0 — initial short shots, gate balance, and basic dimensional check
T1 — full CMM report against drawing tolerances, client review
T2 — process window validation, final parameter lock
First Article Inspection Report issued with approved samples
Step 05 · Engineering
✅
Production Release

Formal gated release with inline inspection protocol in place from day one. Engineering change management active for any subsequent drawing revisions.

PPAP or GP11 documentation package issued on request
Inline inspection plan — frequency, gauges, critical dimensions defined
Engineering change notice (ECN) process established at release
Production control plan linked to process parameters from T2
Step 06 · Engineering
📦
Scaled Dispatch

Controlled volume ramp with lot traceability maintained throughout. Inspection records dispatched with every batch — not on request, by default.

Lot traceability — batch number, machine ID, material cert, and date
Dispatch inspection report attached to each consignment
Volume ramp managed against agreed schedule milestones
Ongoing engineering support and change management post-release

Lot traceability maintained from mill to dispatch

Rishir Shroff

"We didn't build a sales organization. We built an engineering organization that handles its own commercial affairs."

Micron Group was established from a background in automotive product engineering — tooling development, plastic component design, and OEM supply for programs at General Motors, Renault, and Tata. That technical depth is not historical context. It is how we evaluate every project today.

The decision to build in-house tooling capability — rather than outsource it — was deliberate. Control over tooling means control over dimensional consistency, lead times, and engineering change management. It is the difference between a supplier and a manufacturing partner.
